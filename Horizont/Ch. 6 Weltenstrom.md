Arithmetic,  
geography,  
navigation,  
astronomy, etc…  
  
An awful lot for one maid, but it’s necessary to be Elena’s retainer. To be her trusted companion.  
Oktober 29th, 1884, Elena took in a baby today. She named him Leon. They—my family—were already taking a risk when they took me in, but if they ever find out about Leon… We’ll be executed for showing sympathy.  
How could they? To see a little boy in contempt. That kind of thinking is rotten.  
  
*The hull of the ship splits the ocean water.*  
  
Weltenstrom is a rift surrounded by the two region’s cliffs. People carved homes in those cliffs. I’ve also heard they had markets and docks set up. But I can’t see that with this fog. The sun is overhead, its light scattered into the mist. A blinding veil, like snow. Should’ve bought a spyglass when I had the chance…  
Aha! I see some light from a window. That’s pretty weird when I think about it. There’s only one light and I haven’t seen anybody else. Luckily, this building is on the north side—where I’m actually heading. I steer my ship towards the lit building, the scaffolding makes it welcoming to put a ramp down.  
  
I push my hips against the swinging doors of the building.  
  
Looks to be a bar. The bartender has his back towards the door. From what I can see, he looks rough. He puts down a bottle on a shelf as I approach him.  
  
In my very friendly tone—wink, wink. “You got any whiskey?”  
  
He pours me a drink. “You from the east?”  
  
“I’m from up north, actually.” I said as I show him the silver minted from Yurova. “You take these?”  
  
His rough looking hands take the coins. “What’re you doing here?”  
  
“Riches. You know about Alkeluna, right?” I said as I sat down.  
  
“Huh.” He looks at me up and down. ”Y’Best leave... No offense, but you don’t look to trek here.”  
  
“Well… Could you point me to where I can get supplies?”  
  
“You don’t seem to understand. Ever since the war, Alkeluna has been… raided. By something.”  
  
This is news to me. The Supremacists did try to conquer Alkeluna. What about the southern parts? ”Excuse me, what exactly happened with the Supremacists here? Didn’t they fail?”  
  
“I can’t tell... I mean, I don’t know. There’s been rumors of beasts… Villages and settlements having been raided. Destroyed.”  
  
“What?”  
  
“Connect the dots here, boy. The mist, the water. It’s been poisoned. No one knows why.”  
  
“Poisoned? The whole river? Is— Is that why the river seemed empty?”  
  
“Aye, that’s right. Everyone left a while ago, folks returned to their homelands while the natives head back into the deeper parts of Alkeluna.”  
  
A while ago, huh? “Could you give me a specific date? Was there any chance I ran into them on my way here?”  
  
“It’s a bit hazy but if I have to answer, roughly a month ago. So no, you wouldn’t have met anyone.”  
  
The ocean on my way here wasn’t arduous. The people who left must’ve journeyed safely—I hope… I took a drink from my glass. Thinking about it more, I finish my drink.  
  
“You finish drinking now you leave. Out.”  
  
“Hey now! That’s not gonna happen.”  
  
“I said there’s something out there. People have died!”  
  
“Nothings killing me here.” I smugly said to him.”  
  
“What makes you so confident?”  
  
“I’m more than capable. I know exactly what I’m doing and what I’m getting in.”  
  
He furrowed his brow at me. “That’s not proving anything. I’m not letting anyone walk through here.”  
  
I really don’t want to upset this guy. He’s really nice, warning me to leave for my safety. But I won’t back down. Not after coming down here. I have to convince him.  
  
”Haah… Is there anyway I can convince you?”  
  
“No.”  
  
Think about it Nelu. “It took sometime for everyone to leave, right?”  
  
He frowned but he nodded.  
  
“Right. I think. Keyword, *I think* that meant the fishes also had time to leave.”  
  
He treats me with silence but I think he’s listening.  
  
”If the river’s poisoned badly enough to drive off the fish… then whatever’s living off the river should’ve left too. Maybe.”  
  
“You’re drawing from unsure conclusions. If that were true, that means Alkeluna would be a barren wasteland. I’m still not convinced.”  
  
This man… “Then let me ask you something?”  
  
The man kept his silence.  
  
“You’re staying here. For what reason?”  
  
He widens his eyes and seemed to be ruminating on something.  
  
“Well?”  
  
He puts his hands on the counter and looked away from me. “My friend… I’m waiting for my— friend.”  
  
Damn. I hate to take advantage of that. “Would it bring peace to know?”  
  
“Know what? If she’s dead or alive?”  
“N-no. I didn’t mean—”  
  
He a hand off the counter and took heavy wooden steps towards a door. After a while he returns with a bag full of equipment.  
  
“What’re you doing?” I said to him.  
  
“Take my stuff.”  
  
“What?”  
  
“If you’re willing to go then do me a favor.”  
  
“Find your friend?”  
  
“Tall black woman. Shaved head. She carried ornaments. Her name… Awut… Now get the hell out of here.”  
  
”Alright! Geez. Awut was it?”  
The bartender guided me to the stairs leading up to the surface. Everything here was mined and carved out. Agh! The mist was bright enough but the sun doesn’t hold back does it?  
From there I was on my own. Y’know, I’d hoped to have a guide. Someone who can actually walk. Or… someone to huddle against at night. Oh well. Can’t have everything.

*45º forward.*  
  
Degrees now!? Seriously? Okayyy… I’m not complaining.  
  
Rock and sand fill the landscape. The sun heads down while nothing has come to my sight. That doesn’t prove my theory, though. The possibility the there’s something here after—what he described as *beast*—isn’t zero. I’ll gain more insight when the night falls on me, that’s ‘cause the night is when the desert animals go out.   
  
I finish one of the water bottles I was given. “I’m slowly regretting not sleeping for the day.”  
A simple mistake, really. Decided to walk for the remainder of this night then sleep off the midday sun.
